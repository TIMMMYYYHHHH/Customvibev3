**2. Consider quarantine only after evidence passes.** Proposed internal acceptance: every known legitimate route represented; no unexplained legitimate alignment failures; aggregate reports reaching the established destination; forwarding/list usage assessed; operator and rollback record ready. These are proposed project criteria, not a provider guarantee. Review the decision using actual results. Do not rely on `pct=5/25` to contain impact. If quarantine is approved later, retain a full month of representative observation before considering a stricter policy; stop or revert on any known legitimate loss or report gap that prevents assessment.

**3. Do not make reject the default next step.** CustomVibe has a human Workspace mailbox as well as transactional mail. Current RFC 9989, published May 2026 and replacing RFC 7489, removes percentage sampling and highlights indirect-mail risks for general-purpose mail domains. A reject decision needs explicit interoperability evidence and valid aligned DKIM, and may be inappropriate for this mixed-use apex. Keep the current domain/senders; a new subdomain redesign is not proposed. [RFC 9989 sections 7.4 and A.6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9989.html#section-7.4).

**Rollback criteria for any later approved policy change:** on a known legitimate authentication/delivery regression, restore the exact previous working policy promptly; for an enforcement experiment, that will normally be the reviewed reporting-only `p=none` policy, retaining the working `rua`. If the reporting-only edit itself causes a record syntax/processing problem, restore today's exact `v=DMARC1; p=none;` baseline. Recheck that only one DMARC record resolves through both public resolvers after the observed TTL and account for other caches. DNS rollback is not immediate global propagation and does not resend rejected messages. Do not alter MX, DKIM, SPF or application dispatch as an incidental rollback.

Google's current [rollout page](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/security/recommended-dmarc-rollout) still describes percentage staging; the installed deliverability reference does too. This plan deliberately does not treat sampling as a reliable guard because the current IETF specification removed it. DNS publication, historical passes and provider acceptance remain separate from current actual-message alignment and inbox placement.
